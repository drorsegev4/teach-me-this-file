Evaporation and drying

Evaporation is the change of liquid water into water vapor, a gas, at the surface of the liquid. Evaporation can happen below the boiling point; water does not have to boil to evaporate.

With other conditions held equal, warmer water evaporates faster because its particles have more energy and more can escape from the surface into the air.

With other conditions held equal, spreading the same amount of water over a larger exposed surface makes it evaporate faster. More water particles are at the surface and able to escape at once.

For example, a thin wet cloth spread out in a warm room usually dries faster than an equally wet cloth folded up in a cooler room, if airflow and humidity are the same. The water becomes vapor in the air; it has not ceased to exist.
